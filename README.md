## Mission Statement
To eliminate daily campus friction for UBC students by transforming fragmented, hard-to-navigate institutional data into transparent, accessible tools for finding quiet, reliable places to study and collaborate between classes.

---

## Problem Statement

Between lectures, thousands of UBC Vancouver students—over 60% of whom commute to campus ([AMS Academic Experience Survey](https://www.ams.ubc.ca/))—face "dead gaps" of 30 to 180 minutes where they need a quiet, accessible place with seating and power outlets to study, complete assignments, or meet with project teammates ([Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)). However, UBC's primary libraries (Irving K. Barber Learning Centre, Koerner Library, and Woodward Library) and student union spaces (the AMS Nest) routinely reach near-100% seating capacity during peak midday hours (10:00 AM – 4:00 PM), while formal group study rooms must be reserved days or weeks in advance ([Cha & Kim, 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acalib.2015.03.014); [Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)).

Paradoxically, dozens of general-purpose UBC classrooms sit completely empty and unlocked during these exact midday windows ([Beyrouthy et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1057/palgrave.jors.2602523)), and official [UBC Learning Spaces Classroom Usage Guidelines](https://learningspaces.ubc.ca/useful-resources/guidelines/) explicitly permit students to use unbooked classrooms for quiet study when classes or events are not in session. Yet students cannot effectively utilize this surplus capacity due to three systemic barriers ([Beckers et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2015.1123230); [Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)):

1. **Fragmented, Room-by-Room Schedule Data:** UBC's [Online Timetable](https://sws-van.as.it.ubc.ca/) and [Learning Spaces](https://learningspaces.ubc.ca/find-a-space/) pages organize schedules strictly by individual course section or single room code. Answering a simple spatial-temporal question—*"Which classrooms within a 3-minute walk of my path from Kaiser to Buchanan are continuously empty from 11:00 AM to 12:30 PM?"*—requires manually opening and cross-referencing dozens of separate timetable pages while ignoring walking travel times between buildings ([Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)).
2. **Uncertainty Around Room Transitions, Lock-Up Cutoffs, and Unscheduled Closures:** Even when a student finds an apparently empty classroom by walking hallway to hallway, they have no immediate visibility into when the next lecture begins, whether an incoming class needs a 10-minute setup buffer, when the building locks for the evening (which varies from 9:00 PM in MacLeod to 11:00 PM campus-wide), or whether the room has been locked early for an ad-hoc event ([Azizi et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2020.107355)). This creates "eviction anxiety"—the stress of unpacking a laptop only to be displaced minutes later by an arriving instructor ([Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)).
3. **High Coordination Overhead for Small Groups:** When two to four students finishing classes in different buildings across UBC's 400-hectare campus want to meet for an impromptu study or project session, manually intersecting their free timetable blocks while finding a mutually fair, centrally located room with whiteboards and step-free access takes so long that much of their overlapping break is wasted before they even sit down ([Beckers et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2015.1123230); [Harrop & Turpin, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/13614533.2013.740961)).

As a consequence, students lose productive study time wandering hallways, crowd onto noisy hallway floors and stairwells without desk space or power outlets, and suffer increased physical fatigue and reduced sense of belonging compared to peers with private on-campus residence rooms ([Pokorny et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-016-0063-3); [Regalado & Smale, 2015](https://doi.org/10.5860/crl.76.7.899)).
